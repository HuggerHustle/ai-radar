## 3. Market Signal

**Agent operations is today's most crowded category.** Seven of fifteen launches — Boost.space AgentOS, OpenBot, OpenFrame, ChatGPT Wallet, Puffin, StateWeave, ByteVoice, and UI SDK — are aimed at people *building or running* agents, not end users. This is a classic picks-and-shovels moment: the ecosystem is maturing faster than the agents themselves, and startups are racing to own identity, payment, testing, and UI layers before the market consolidates.

**The most novel approaches** are **ChatGPT Wallet** (scoped spending credentials so agents can transact without exposing card data) and **StateWeave** (testing agents against cloned, stateful copies of the apps they touch). Both attack the same real-world blocker: agents are easy to demo and hard to trust in production.

**Open-source is a strong undercurrent** — roughly a third of launches (OpenBot, Cookie Away, UI SDK, Punchcutter, plus GitHub-tagged Puffin) ship with open code, and several market themselves explicitly as self-hosted alternatives to closed incumbents. Meanwhile, incumbents (Zoom, Cal.com, ChatGPT, Vercel) are embedding AI features into existing distribution rather than launching standalone apps — a split between platform-led features and infra-led startups.
